## The System

Jazz inverts funk's variation budget: the *patterns* vary continuously while the *frame* — cycle length (the song form), tempo, and two fixed voices — holds. The **ride cymbal pattern** is the timeline (constant in rhythm, endlessly inflected in touch), the **hi-hat foot on 2 and 4** its shadow; above them, **comping** (snare/kick accents, piano chords) is genuinely responsive — placed in real time against what the soloist plays, drawn from a learned vocabulary rather than composed in advance ([Berliner 1994](/appendix-references/#fr-berliner-1994); [Monson 1996](/appendix-references/#fr-monson-1996)). The counterpoint is literal conversation: statements, answers, interruptions — but disciplined by the form's bar-structure and cadence points.

The feel layer is quantifiably strict: swing ratio is a *function of tempo* (wide at slow tempi, near-even at extreme speed — [Friberg & Sundström 2002](/appendix-references/#fr-friberg-2002)), soloists bend eighth-note placement expressively within the beat ([Benadon 2006](/appendix-references/#fr-benadon-2006)), and the rhythm section's members deliberately do *not* align exactly — the small, stable timing discrepancies between ride, bass, and soloist are constitutive of the swing feel, not flaws — Keil's "participatory discrepancies" ([Keil 1987](/appendix-references/#fr-keil-1987); [Prögler 1995](/appendix-references/#fr-progler-1995)).

## The Rules

1. **The ride keeps the time, and only the ride keeps the time.** The ride pattern's rhythm is constant (its dynamics breathe); nothing else is obliged to state the pulse. Once the ride is trusted, kick and snare are *freed from timekeeping* — that liberation is the whole design ([Berliner 1994](/appendix-references/#fr-berliner-1994)).

2. **Hi-hat 2 and 4, planted.** The foot hat is the style's backbeat-ghost: quiet, constant, structural. (Modern post-bop loosens this deliberately; the default idiom keeps it.)

3. **Comping answers; it does not run.** Comping figures are short (one to four attacks), placed in the *gaps* of the melodic line, echoing or counter-accenting it, then yielding. Continuous busy comping is the classic student error — the vocabulary is call-and-response at the sub-phrase level ([Monson 1996](/appendix-references/#fr-monson-1996); [Riley 1994](/appendix-references/#fr-riley-1994) catalogues the figures).

4. **Nothing repeats verbatim, nothing is unrelated.** Consecutive comping figures vary — displaced, inverted, extended — but draw on shared cells so the stream is coherent. This is mutation *with memory* (theme-and-variation at high rate), the exact opposite of funk's riff-lock. In Poly: sustained moderate mutation (15–30%) on the comping lanes is the honest approximation.

5. **The form is the meter.** Phrasing is organised by the chorus structure (12-bar, 32-bar AABA) and its 4- and 8-bar sub-units: comping intensity marks section boundaries, fills signal turnarounds, and the top of each chorus functions like sam — a convergence everyone aims at. A rhythm section that never marks the form is "running the time," not playing the tune.

6. **Swing ratio follows tempo; voices' ratios differ slightly.** Long-short ratio ~3:1 at ballad tempi, narrowing toward 1:1 beyond ~250 BPM, with the short note roughly constant in absolute duration ([Friberg & Sundström 2002](/appendix-references/#fr-friberg-2002)). Ride, hat, and comping voices swing at *slightly different* ratios (per-lane swing in Poly), and the section's members sit at small stable offsets from each other ([Prögler 1995](/appendix-references/#fr-progler-1995)) — configure per-lane swing differences of 0.05–0.1 and per-lane timing offsets of 1–3 ms.

7. **Superimpositions borrow, then repay.** Elvin Jones-style cross-rhythms (dotted-quarter 3-over-4 layers, 5- and 7-groupings) may run for bars, but they resolve at a form boundary (Rule 5) — the same loan-repayment logic as the tala page's cross-groupings. An unresolved superimposition is modern free practice, not the core idiom.

8. **Dynamics are inverted relative to rock/funk.** The ride and hat are the loudest *constant* voices; snare and kick live mostly below them, surfacing for accents. A backbeat-heavy mix reads instantly as rock.

## Construction

1. **Ride lane.** 4 steps at 1/4, 4 hits (the quarter framework; the skip-beat lives in the swing), swing 0.45 at medium tempo, mutation 0 (Rules 1, 6).
2. **Hat lane.** 4 steps, 2 hits, rotation to 2 and 4, quiet, mutation 0 (Rule 2).
3. **Snare comp lane.** 16 steps at 1/16, 3–4 hits (Rule 3 caps a comping figure at four attacks), mutation 25%, ghost 40, swing 0.35 (Rules 3, 4, 6).
4. **Kick comp lane.** 8 steps, 2–3 hits, low velocity ("feathered" or accent-only), mutation 15%, swing 0.4.
5. **Form marking.** FillLikelihood envelope with an 8-bar period peaking at the phrase end (Rule 5); optionally a scene chain alternating "head" and "solo" intensities.
6. **Optional superimposition lane.** 3- or 6-step cycle, phrase-gated to run for a stretch and fall silent at a form boundary (Rule 7).

## What Breaks the Idiom

- **A varying ride.** Mutation on lane 1 removes the trusted clock that licenses everything else (Rule 1).
- **Riff-locked comping.** Zero mutation on the comp lanes produces a funk drummer playing a jazz chart (Rule 4).
- **Formlessness.** No fill/intensity contour at 4/8-bar boundaries — endless texture with no tune (Rule 5).
- **Uniform swing.** Identical ratio on every lane, unchanged across tempi (Rule 6).
- **Backbeat weight.** Loud 2/4 snare (Rule 8).
- **Perpetual superimposition.** Cross-rhythms that never resolve (Rule 7).
