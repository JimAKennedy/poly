The research and analysis in this guide draws on ethnomusicological scholarship, mathematical rhythm theory, and music production science. References are grouped by the chapter where they are first cited.

Nothing in this guide — including the [Theory Deep Dives](/theory-counterpoint-overview/) section — is original research. The guide summarises and re-organises the published work below, restated in Poly's vocabulary for practical use; where a deep-dive page compresses ensemble practice into a rule of thumb that no single source states, the page's Attribution note says so explicitly.

## Further Reading

A curated bibliography beyond the numbered citations above, grouped by the chapter each source deepens. These are the works to consult for the music theory behind idiomatic counterpoint in each tradition.

### Foundations and Cross-Cultural Rhythm Theory (Chapter 1)

- <span id="fr-toussaint-2013">Toussaint, G. T. (2013). *The Geometry of Musical Rhythm: What Makes a "Good" Rhythm Good?* CRC Press.</span>
- <span id="fr-toussaint-2005">Toussaint, G. T. (2005). "The Euclidean Algorithm Generates Traditional Musical Rhythms." *BRIDGES Conference*.</span>
- <span id="fr-london-2012">London, J. (2012). *Hearing in Time: Psychological Aspects of Musical Meter*, 2nd ed. Oxford University Press. — The theory of non-isochronous meters; the theoretical frame behind both aksak and tala.</span>
- <span id="fr-pressing-2002">Pressing, J. (2002). "Black Atlantic Rhythm: Its Computational and Transcultural Foundations." *Music Perception*, 19(3). — Why the shared timelines of the Black Atlantic have common cognitive properties.</span>

### Sub-Saharan Africa (Chapter 2)

- <span id="fr-arom-1991">Arom, S. (1991). *African Polyphony and Polyrhythm: Musical Structure and Methodology*. Cambridge University Press.</span>
- <span id="fr-locke-1998">Locke, D. (1998). *Drum Gahu: An Introduction to African Rhythm*. White Cliffs Media.</span>
- <span id="fr-locke-1982">Locke, D. (1982). "Principles of Offbeat Timing and Cross-Rhythm in Southern Ewe Dance Drumming." *Ethnomusicology*, 26(2). — How support parts are placed *against* the bell timeline.</span>
- <span id="fr-chernoff-1979">Chernoff, J. M. (1979). *African Rhythm and African Sensibility*. University of Chicago Press. — The ensemble-social logic behind timeline, support, and lead roles.</span>
- <span id="fr-anku-2000">Anku, W. (2000). "Circles and Time: A Theory of Structural Organization of Rhythm in African Music." *Music Theory Online*, 6(1). — A circular, set-theoretic model of African rhythm close in spirit to Poly's cycle model.</span>
- <span id="fr-polak-2010">Polak, R. (2010). "Rhythmic Feel as Meter: Non-Isochronous Beat Subdivision in Jembe Music from Mali." *Music Theory Online*, 16(4). — Measured microtiming showing jembe subdivision is systematically uneven.</span>
- <span id="fr-polak-london-2014">Polak, R. & London, J. (2014). "Timing and Meter in Mande Drumming from Mali." *Music Theory Online*, 20(1).</span>
- <span id="fr-novotney-1998">Novotney, E. (1998). "The 3:2 Relationship as the Foundation of Timelines in West African Musics." *PhD dissertation, University of Illinois*.</span>
- <span id="fr-agawu-2006">Agawu, K. (2006). "Structural Analysis or Cultural Analysis? Competing Perspectives on the 'Standard Pattern' of West African Rhythm." *Journal of the American Musicological Society*, 59(1).</span>

### Afro-Cuban (Chapter 3)

- <span id="fr-uribe-1996">Uribe, E. (1996). *The Essence of Afro-Cuban Percussion and Drum Set*. Warner Bros. — Practical part-by-part idiom: cascara, tumbao, conga marcha.</span>
- <span id="fr-washburne-1997">Washburne, C. (1997). "The Clave of Jazz: A Caribbean Contribution to the Rhythmic Foundation of an African-American Music." *Black Music Research Journal*, 17(1).</span>

### Gamelan (Chapter 5)
