The research and analysis in this guide draws on ethnomusicological scholarship, mathematical rhythm theory, and music production science. References are grouped by the chapter where they are first cited.

Nothing in this guide is original research. The guide summarises and re-organises the published work
below, restated in Poly's vocabulary for practical use; where a chapter compresses ensemble practice
into a rule of thumb that no single source states, it says so explicitly.

### Sub-Saharan Africa (Chapter 2)

- <span id="fr-locke-1982" data-tier="A">Locke, D. (1982). "Principles of Offbeat Timing and Cross-Rhythm in Southern Ewe Dance Drumming." *Ethnomusicology*, 26(2), 217–246. [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/851524) — How support parts are placed *against* the bell timeline.</span>
- <span id="fr-charry-2000" data-tier="A">Charry, E. (2000). *Mande Music: Traditional and Modern Music of the Maninka and Mandinka of Western Africa*. University of Chicago Press. — Jembe drumming is one of its four surveyed spheres; cited for what Manding ensemble practice actually is, against the guide's same-cycle simplification.</span>
- <span id="fr-kubik-1999" data-tier="A">Kubik, G. (1999). *Africa and the Blues*. University Press of Mississippi. — Traces African rhythmic retentions through the Western and Central Sudanic belt; cited for the thread running from Chapter 2 to Chapter 4.</span>
- <span id="fr-agawu-2003" data-tier="A">Agawu, K. (2003). *Representing African Music: Postcolonial Notes, Queries, Positions*. Routledge. — A critique of how African music is written about and by whom. Distinct from Agawu (2006) below, which argues a narrower methodological point about structural versus cultural analysis; cited where the guide frames the African–minimalist connection.</span>

### Afro-Cuban (Chapter 3)

- <span id="fr-acosta-2003" data-tier="A">Acosta, L. (2003). *Cubano Be, Cubano Bop: One Hundred Years of Jazz in Cuba*. Smithsonian Books. — Covers the Cuban presence in New Orleans and the "Spanish tinge" in early jazz there; cited for the historical depth behind how clave practice travelled.</span>
- <span id="fr-penalosa-2009" data-tier="A">Peñalosa, D. (2009). *The Clave Matrix: Afro-Cuban Rhythm — Its Principles and African Origins*. Bembe Books. — The definitive treatment of clave alignment rules.</span>
- <span id="fr-mauleon-1993" data-tier="A">Mauleón, R. (1993). *Salsa Guidebook for Piano and Ensemble*. Sher Music.</span>
- <span id="fr-lomax-1950" data-tier="A">Lomax, A. (1950). *Mister Jelly Roll: The Fortunes of Jelly Roll Morton, New Orleans Creole and "Inventor of Jazz"*. Duell, Sloan and Pearce. — The Library of Congress interviews in which Morton describes the "Spanish tinge".</span>

### Afrobeat (Chapter 4)

### Gamelan (Chapter 5)

- <span id="fr-tenzer-2000" data-tier="A">Tenzer, M. (2000). *Gamelan Gong Kebyar: The Art of Twentieth-Century Balinese Music*. University of Chicago Press. — The authoritative analysis of kotekan varieties.</span>
- <span id="fr-vitale-1990" data-tier="A">Vitale, W. (1990). "Kotekan: The Technique of Interlocking Parts in Balinese Music." *Balungan*, 4(2). [PDF](https://gamelan.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/v_4_2_Kotekan_the_technique_of_interlocking_parts_in_Balinese_music_Vitale.pdf) — Short and practical; shows real kotekan is not a pure set complement.</span>

### Indian Classical (Chapter 6)

- <span id="fr-powers-1980" data-tier="A">Qureshi, R., Powers, H. S., Katz, J., Widdess, R. et al. (2001). "India, subcontinent of". *Grove Music Online*. Oxford University Press. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/gmo/9781561592630.article.43272) — The standard survey of Indian art-music theory across the subcontinent; cited for the wider frame this guide narrows from, and for its "Rhythm and tāla" section in particular. This is the 2001 second-edition rewrite of Powers's 1980 *New Grove* article of the same title, which the anchor still names; the rewrite is the version a reader can reach.</span>
- <span id="fr-clayton-2000" data-tier="A">Clayton, M. (2000). *Time in Indian Music: Rhythm, Metre, and Form in North Indian Rāg Performance*. Oxford University Press. — The scholarly standard on tala, laya, and sam.</span>
- <span id="fr-nelson-2008" data-tier="A">Nelson, D. (2008). *Solkattu Manual*. Wesleyan University Press. — Carnatic rhythmic construction, including the tihai/mora arithmetic.</span>
- <span id="fr-kippen-1988" data-tier="A">Kippen, J. (1988). *The Tabla of Lucknow*. Cambridge University Press. — Theka elaboration practice.</span>

### Balkan (Chapter 7)

- <span id="fr-peycheva-dimov-2002" data-tier="B">Peycheva, L. &amp; Dimov, V. (2002). *The Zurna Tradition in Southwest Bulgaria: Romani Musicians in Practice*. Bŭlgarsko muzikoznanie. — Existence, authors, year, title and series confirmed from catalogue records; **contents unverified**, the work being in Bulgarian and no translation yet obtained. Listed because the scholarship on Romani professional practice in Bulgaria is relevant to Chapter 7's subject; no claim in this guide currently rests on it.</span>
- <span id="fr-silverman-2007" data-tier="A">Silverman, C. (2007). "Bulgarian Wedding Music between Folk and Chalga: Politics, Markets and Current Directions". *Muzikologija*. — Traces svatbarska muzika from its suppression under state socialism to its post-socialist decline against chalga; cited for the tradition's history rather than its metrics.</span>
- <span id="fr-brailoiu-1951" data-tier="A">Brăiloiu, C. (1951). "Le rythme aksak." *Revue de Musicologie*, 33(99/100), 71–108. [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/926002) — The origin of the term and the two/three-cell theory of additive meter.</span>
- <span id="fr-rice-1994" data-tier="A">Rice, T. (1994). *May It Fill Your Soul: Experiencing Bulgarian Music*. University of Chicago Press.</span>
- <span id="fr-goldberg-2015" data-tier="A">Goldberg, D. (2015). "Timing Variations in Two Balkan Percussion Performances." *Empirical Musicology Review*, 10(4). — Measured evidence that aksak "long" beats are not exactly 3:2.</span>

### Electronic and Drum & Bass (Chapters 9, 13)

- <span id="fr-harrison-2025" data-tier="A">Harrison, N. (2025). "Reflections on the Amen Break: A Continued History, an Unsettled Ethics". In *The Routledge Companion to Remix Studies*, 2nd ed., eds. E. Navas, O. Gallagher &amp; x. burrough. Routledge. [Book](https://www.routledge.com/The-Routledge-Companion-to-Remix-Studies/Navas-Gallagher-burrough/p/book/9781032509105) · [Chapter DOI](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003402459-49) — By the maker of the 2004 work that first documented the break's history; cited for that history and for the sampling ethics around it, not for a count of uses.</span>
- <span id="fr-linn-attack-2020" data-tier="B">Linn, R., interviewed in *Attack Magazine* (2020). "Roger Linn On Swing, Groove &amp; The Magic Of The MPC's Timing". — Linn describing the LM-1 mechanism in his own words: quantise each beat to the nearest step, then delay the playback of every other step. Tier B as a professional publication interviewing the primary actor rather than scholarship; cited in preference to commentary quoting it second-hand.</span>
- <span id="fr-butler-2006" data-tier="A">Butler, M. J. (2006). *Unlocking the Groove: Rhythm, Meter, and Musical Design in Electronic Dance Music*. Indiana University Press.</span>
