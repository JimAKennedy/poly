Landed as one commit rather than the plan's four tasks. The plan warned this
might be necessary — its task 1 produces a case that stays red until the
resolution lands — and the finding below made the split worse, not better: the
work it describes is one change, not three.

**B08's premise was largely wrong, and measuring is what showed it.** The row
counted eighteen numbered references and two Further Reading entries as cited by
no page, and called an uncited entry "either dead weight or a source nobody
checked". Seventeen of the twenty-one were neither. They sat inside range
listings — `See also refs [21]–[25]` — which hyperlink only their endpoints, so
`ref-22`, `ref-23` and `ref-24` were pointed at in prose while being unreachable
by anchor. Ten such ranges existed, one per theory page, covering refs 4–43
almost continuously.

Applied literally, the plan's policy would have deleted or re-homed seventeen
references that a theory page already names in its own Sources line, including
the Indian-classical further reading `theory-indian-classical.mdx` points at.

The ranges are expanded into explicit links instead — about thirty new anchors,
purely mechanical — rather than teaching the check to parse `refs [a]–[b]`. A
checker that understands a prose convention is a checker that breaks when the
prose changes, and the expansion also makes every entry clickable, which the
range never did.
