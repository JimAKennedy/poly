B09's duplicate — `ref-1` and `fr-toussaint-2005`, the same 2005 BRIDGES paper —
was already retired in M006/S02, where `fr-toussaint-2005` also appeared as an
orphan. So this slice's work was the check, and the check found a second
duplicate the row never named.

**`ref-5` and `fr-arom-1991` were both Arom's *African Polyphony and Polyrhythm:
Musical Structure and Methodology* (1991), Cambridge University Press.** That is
the sweep B09's `Verification` asks to be recorded: 108 entries compared, two
collisions, one of them unknown to the row.

`fr-arom-1991` was kept — it is cited from two pages, including the methodology
claim M005/S01 added — and `ref-5` retired.

**Retiring it broke a live claim, and the tier check caught it.** `ref-5` was
referenced twice from `theory-sub-saharan-africa.mdx`: once in the Sources
listing M006/S02 expanded, and once as a superscript claim citation on line 13,
for the timeline functioning as the ensemble's shared clock. A `grep` for
`#ref-5)` showed one file and read as one use. Deleting the entry left that
claim citing nothing, and `citation-tier.test.mjs` failed with
`theory-sub-saharan-africa.mdx:13 cites ref-5 (tier undeclared)`. The claim now
cites `fr-arom-1991`, which is the same work.

Worth stating plainly: the mistake was treating "which files cite this" as
"how many citations exist". The check that caught it is one M002 built.

The duplicate check compares per line, not per span. A numbered entry wraps only
its bracketed number in a `<span>` and carries its bibliography on the rest of
the line, while a Further Reading entry wraps the lot — a span-spanning regex
reads across neighbouring entries and invents collisions, which the first
version did, reporting `ref-5 and fr-arom-1991` for the wrong reason before
reporting it for the right one.

It keys on year plus the first six significant title words, because publisher
and URL differ between a DOI and a PDF of the same paper. That is how the
Toussaint pair hid: an exact-string comparison would have found nothing and
reported success.

Proved to bite: re-adding a duplicate Toussaint entry under a new anchor failed
the case naming both, removed by inverse edit.
