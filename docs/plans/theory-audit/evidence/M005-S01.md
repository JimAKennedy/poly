Four citations, each bound in its case to a phrase from the passage it belongs
to rather than to the page. All four were watched to fail before being written.

**F52's premise was half stale, and the half that remained is the interesting
one.** `theory-sub-saharan-africa.mdx` already carried Arom under `## Sources`
as `Primary: [Arom 1991] (referent theory)`. That is a listing, not a citation
at a claim — the distinction M002 built the tier check around. The dance-beat
sentence, "a second referent that Western transcription usually omits", is
precisely the methodology claim Arom's referent theory supports, and it now
names him there.

**F45 required a judgment call, recorded in the decisions file.** The row says
Kubik covers "the African rhythmic retentions that bridge Ch 2 and Ch 4", but
Chapter 2 has no such passage — its sections are the gankogui bell, role
separation, polymetric stacking, Manding ensembles and improvisatory feel.
Writing one to host the citation is what this milestone's plans forbid. Kubik is
instead cited at the opening's claim that the subject is "the polymetric
drumming of West and Central Africa", which his survey of the Western and
Central Sudanic belt directly supports, with the diaspora thread mentioned as
what Chapter 4 picks up rather than asserted as a new claim.

**One case bound to the wrong occurrence and was corrected, not loosened.** F45
first matched the phrase "West and Central Africa" in the frontmatter
description, whose 600-character window contains no citation, so the case failed
with the citation present. The fix was to bind to the body sentence — "it is the
polymetric drumming of West and Central Africa" — which is unique. Widening the
window would have made the case pass by reaching material it was never meant to
see. It was re-proved to bite afterwards.

The site build was run because the citations sit inside an MDX `:::note` and a
frontmatter-adjacent paragraph, and a malformed link there breaks the page
rather than a test.
