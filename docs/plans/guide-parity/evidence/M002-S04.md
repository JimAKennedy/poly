A response lane's gate is the complement of its call's, with a lead-in shifting
its view of the call's clock: positive opens early (anticipation), negative
opens late (dovetail). Both signs are asserted, because one would pass by
magnitude alone.

**The case that carries the claim** is
`SurvivesAChangeToTheSourcesPhraseLength`. That is the failure the manual recipe
has — change one lane's phrase settings and the antiphony breaks silently — and
it is the reason the row exists.

**The reuse that justified this slice's grouping is real.** The reference is
resolved with `referenceLaneUsable`, the same guard M002/S01 built for the
timeline reference. Nothing was written twice.

**But the guard is defensive, not load-bearing, and the probe proved it.**
Removing it leaves `AMutualReferenceIsRefused` green. What actually prevents
recursion is the implementation reading the source's **own** phrase gate rather
than its response gate: the recursion has nowhere to go. Rather than contrive a
case that would fail, a new case pins the property doing the real work —
`ReadsTheSourcesOwnGateNotItsResponseGate` — so that someone later "simplifying"
`responseGateOpen` to consult the source's response gate would introduce
unbounded recursion and be caught by a test rather than by a stack overflow.

| Probe | Result |
|---|---|
| the gate ignores `responseSource` | `TheTwoLanesNeverOverlap` **fails** |
| the mutual-reference guard removed | nothing fails — recorded above rather than papered over |
