### This is "automation found none", not "none exists"

That distinction is the one this programme exists to keep, and it is why the
task stops here rather than proceeding to reword six claims.

Four of the six — Locke, Brăiloiu, Cohn, Powers — are paywalled **by
publication model** rather than by bot-blocking, so a browser alone does not
change them. But the owner holds **Academia.edu and Scribd accounts**, which is
exactly where reposted copies of canonical articles like Cohn 1992 and Locke
1982 tend to sit, and neither is reachable from automation. `ref-9` and `ref-22`
in the verifiable-references programme were both settled that way after
automation had given up.

Recording "no obtainable copy exists" on this evidence would be the same
overclaim that VR02 made about `ref-22` — *"the PDF moved rather than the source
being bad"* — which re-measurement disproved.

**The retrieval attempt is committed on its own**, before any editorial
decision, so the research and the decisions it drives are separable in review.
