### The guard is the generalised form of M001's, and it is not dominated

M001/S01's queue guard hardcoded one host and one anchor, and was dominated by
`REF22-CLAYTON` the moment that host was forbidden tree-wide. This one is keyed
on the manifest's own verdicts, so it applies to every entry reaching the same
state. Proved in three directions:

| mutation | result |
|---|---|
| a queued row is deleted from the worklist | **red**, naming the entry |
| an entry newly becomes browser-only and unverified | **red**, naming the entry |
| an entry leaves the state (verdict becomes `verified`) | green — the obligation correctly lifts |

The middle case is the one that matters and the one M001's guard could not do:
nothing else in the suite notices an entry *entering* the queueable state, so
without this the 20th unreachable entry would be recorded and never queued.

The pairing is deliberately `browser-only` **and** `unverified`, not `unverified`
alone. An entry unverified because it is purchasable or library-only cannot be
settled by a browser session, and queueing it would fill the worklist with work
nobody can do.
